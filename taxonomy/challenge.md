# Taxonomy challenge

Challenger lens: a primary-care or global-health clinician working in a low-resource setting (community health workers, nurses and clinical officers in health posts and health centres; patients who pay out of pocket and may be hours from a hospital).

Read against `taxonomy/proposal.yaml` (12 domains, 73 constructs) and `search/work/cluster_digest.txt`, with term lists checked in `taxonomy/terms.jsonl`.

One pattern runs through several objections below. Benchmarks built in or for LMICs name their context qualities in their own words ("Understanding of local context", "Resource Alignment", "Suitability for a rural African primary care context"), so their terms end up in singleton or 2-entry clusters. Those clusters were then either left unassigned or attached to constructs they do not fit. The context constructs look thin because of how the clusters formed, not because few evaluations measure them. A second pattern is that several constructs define success in terms that assume high-income health systems: calling 911, an emergency department, direct specialist access, a full diagnostic workup, Medicare prices.

Objections are ordered by how much they would change what a low-resource clinician takes from the taxonomy.

---

## 1. Local context fit is under-counted and partly mis-mapped

- **Construct:** `local_context_fit`
- **Objection:** Three of the eleven clusters cited for it measure something else. k1974 ("Platform-vs-local concordance", turkish-postpartum-care-benchmark) is agreement between two scoring pipelines, a property of the evaluation. k994 (haicef "Cost-effectiveness", "Comparative effectiveness") is the deployed system's value compared with existing care, not whether advice is feasible. k1662 ("Medical Care for All", medsafetybench) is an AMA ethics principle. k404 is mixed: "Local accuracy" means alignment with *hospital policy*, and its other two terms (medconsultbench Hit@1, FQR) concern question order in a consultation. Meanwhile the clearest evidence is unassigned or sits elsewhere: HealthBench's "Global health" theme (k1001: "adapt responses ... across differences in resource availability, clinical norms, and regional patterns of disease"), nigbench "Appropriateness: Suitability for a rural African primary care context" (k1744), alama "Rationale Localization" (k1189: endemic diseases, policy), medpi "patient context" (k797: socioeconomic, cultural, environmental factors), nigbench "Bias: suggestions inappropriate or irrelevant to local contexts" (k1746, currently under fairness), and the Kenya arm of sim-ut "Scope Adherence" (k1127: "stay within the user's constraints, such as limited food options", currently under scope). Once these are counted, the construct is supported by about ten entries (rwanda-chw-vignettes, kenya-nurse-vignettes, afrimed-qa, alama-health-qa, medpi, gpbench, healthbench, nigbench, sim-ut, plus HealthBench-style "context awareness" in livemedbench and kampobench).
- **Proposed change:** Remove k1974 (to `evaluator_reliability`), k994 (to `user_outcomes`) and k1662 (to `ethical_conduct`). Keep only the "Local accuracy" term of k404 here, as a narrower term. Add k1001 (Global health term only), k1744, k1746, k1189, k797 (patient context and system coordination terms), k1127 (Kenya term), k1187 ("Formulation Fit", e.g. pentavalent vs hexavalent) and k935 ("Supportive services ... transport and logistical needs"). Add k048 as partial: HealthBench, kampobench and livemedbench define context awareness as responding to "geographic setting, resources the user says they have". Broaden the definition to name the dimensions evaluations actually check: availability of medicines, formulations, diagnostics and services; cost to the patient; distance and transport; cold chain; local epidemiology (endemic disease). Keep it in `equity_context`.
- **Evidence:** k635, k871, k920, k1054, k1185, k1188, k1418, k1001, k1744, k1746, k1189, k797, k1127, k1187, k935, k048; mis-mapped: k1974, k994, k1662, k404.

## 2. National and local protocol adherence is a different quality from guideline concordance

- **Construct:** new, `national_protocol_adherence` (split from `guideline_adherence`)
- **Objection:** `guideline_adherence` mixes two things that evaluations score separately. Most of its terms check concordance with a named international or specialty guideline (GINA in asthma-consumer-llm-safety-vignettes k925/k926, NICE in nice-guideline-silver-standard k081, a standard of care in breast-oncology-vignettes k734). A distinct group checks concordance with the protocol that is *in force in a given jurisdiction*: "Guideline grounding: does the model answer from the correct guideline (e.g., ADA vs. AHA vs. IAP) rather than generic medical knowledge?" (indian-protocols-clinical-qna k334), "off-protocol recommendations" as a negative criterion (k188), entity overlap with Kazakh national protocols (k372, k139), exact Uganda Clinical Guidelines treatment and dosing (uccb-health k713), "Uganda-specific clinical decision support" (k1977, unassigned), Brazil's Dietary Guidelines (k934), Turkish postpartum guideline knowledge (k1975), and the national immunization schedule (alama k1186 "Antigen Accuracy", unassigned). For a clinician in Uganda or Kazakhstan, an answer that matches US or European guidance but contradicts the national standard treatment guideline (first-line drug, regimen, dosing band, vaccine formulation) is wrong in practice and often not stocked. Folding both into one construct means a high "guideline adherence" score can hide exactly this failure. The same tension affects `knowledge_currency`: the newest international recommendation can conflict with the national protocol still in force.
- **Proposed change:** Create `national_protocol_adherence` in `correctness`: "Recommendations follow the national or institutional protocol that applies in the user's setting, including choosing the right jurisdiction's guideline when several exist." Move k334, k372 and k139 (Kazakh protocol terms), k713 (UCG term only; its rarebench term is screening recall), k934, k1975, k1977, k1186, and the "Local accuracy" term of k404. Map k188's indian-protocols term as partial. Cross-link k333 (retrieval "publisher selection" in indian-protocols) and k1918 (citations to "country-government ... WHO ... national health-service" sites) as partial. Keep `guideline_adherence` for concordance with a named international or specialty guideline, and require mapped terms to record which guideline is the reference.
- **Evidence:** k334, k188, k372, k139, k713, k1977, k934, k1975, k1186, k404, k333, k1918; contrast k925, k926, k081, k734.

## 3. Emergency escalation is written for settings with ambulances and 24-hour EDs, and ignores over-escalation

- **Construct:** `emergency_escalation`
- **Objection:** Two problems. (a) The pass criteria in the main clusters are phrased as specific services: "contact emergency services / attend an ED / call an ambulance now" (asthma Flag 1, k042), "mentions ER but omits calling 911 for STEMI" counted as incomplete escalation (lostbench k437), "call 911, go to the ER" (risk-bearing-language-rubric k042). Where there is no working ambulance number and the nearest hospital is hours away, the right advice is to convey urgency and give a feasible route to the nearest facility that can treat the condition. A model that says "call 911" to a user in rural Malawi passes these rubrics and fails the patient. (b) The definition only penalizes under-escalation ("without false reassurance or downplaying risk"), yet the clusters already score the other direction: "escalation ... if and only if indicated" (k028), "avoiding unnecessary escalation in non-urgent scenarios" (livemedbench k042), "over_alarmism_and_under_response" (k196), "Over-escalation on defer scenarios" (k1528), and an explicit false-negative/false-positive cost matrix (k2005). Where one unnecessary trip costs a day's wages and transport, over-escalation is a real harm. Lostbench's "Recognition" and "Persistence" (holding the recommendation across turns of patient pressure) and the asthma "Follow-up probe rescue" sit unassigned, although they are the core of this construct.
- **Proposed change:** Redefine as "Recognizes red-flag or danger-sign presentations, conveys the right urgency, and directs the user to the nearest care that can act, holding that advice under pushback, without escalating cases that do not need it." State that rubrics naming a specific service (911, ambulance, ED) are narrower terms valid only where that service exists. Add k1526, k1527, k1195 and k1904 (sense-and-sensitivity-triage "unnecessary escalations"). Name danger-sign recognition explicitly: k191's uccb-health "danger_sign" and turkish-postpartum "danger signs and referral" labels and gpbench "Acute and Critical Condition Recognition" (k548) are the LMIC form of red-flag recognition.
- **Evidence:** k042, k437, k028, k196, k1528, k2005, k1525, k1526, k1527, k1195, k1904, k191, k548.

## 4. Triage accuracy merges ED acuity scales with care-level disposition

- **Construct:** `triage_accuracy` (split; new `care_level_disposition`)
- **Objection:** About half of the construct is emergency-department acuity classification against ESI or Manchester scales and ED disposition: er-reason ESI accuracy (k311), acuitybench, ehr2dial and mts-bench under- and over-triage (k052, k146, k312, k461, k462), "Disposition accuracy: discharge, admit, observe, transfer" (k1355). The other half is the decision a patient or frontline worker actually faces: self-care vs non-emergency vs emergency care (repvig k231, best-self-triage k778), "Manage / Visit / Resource" (sense-and-sensitivity-triage k908), and, filed under `referral_appropriateness` and `treatment_management`, "Miss Refer" vs "Miss GP (missed local management) — an unnecessary referral" (gpagentbench k987) and "treat ... or refer" (k609). Accuracy on ESI levels in a US ED says little about whether a chatbot correctly tells a mother in a village that her child can be managed at home, needs the health centre today, or needs the district hospital now. Manage-here-or-refer-up is also the central decision for a community health worker.
- **Proposed change:** Rename the ED part `acuity_classification` (clinical_reasoning), limited to scored acuity scales and ED disposition (k311, k052, k146, k312, k461, k462, k1355). Create `care_level_disposition` (safety): "Recommends the right level of care for the situation, from self-care through primary care to hospital, including managing locally when that is safe, with under- and over-referral tracked as separate errors." Move k231 (repvig and patientagentbench terms), k778, k908, k966, k987, k609 and k1904. Keep `emergency_escalation` for recognizing time-critical danger and acting on it.
- **Evidence:** k311, k052, k146, k312, k461, k462, k1355; k231, k778, k908, k966, k987, k609, k1904.

## 5. Referral appropriateness assumes direct specialist access and credits generic signposting

- **Construct:** `referral_appropriateness`
- **Objection:** Much of the construct is routing to a hospital department or specialist: "Specialty referral accuracy" and "Department Accuracy" (k488), "Correct Specialty Identified" (k1204), cp-env "Triage Precision: appropriateness of recommended medical departments" (k231). That task presumes a system where patients can walk into a specialty outpatient department. In most LMIC systems, referral goes up a tier (health post, health centre, district hospital), and what matters first is whether to refer at all (objection 4). Crisis-resource terms are calibrated to one country: "Correct, locale-appropriate crisis resources (e.g., 988)" (distressbench k125). Several terms give partial credit to vague signposting ("0.5 vague referral", cci-iwri k503), although others score it as a failure ("Generic referral language without naming a specific resource", k1770; asthma "Flag 8: Pure refusal / redirect", k112). "See a doctor" is close to useless advice where doctors are scarce.
- **Proposed change:** Define the construct as *destination and signposting*: "Points the user to a specific, reachable service, support or resource that exists in their setting." Move the whether-to-refer terms to `care_level_disposition`. Mark department and specialty routing (k488, k1204, cp-env term of k231) as a narrower, hospital-navigation term. State that crisis-resource terms (k125) are valid only for resources that exist and are staffed in the user's country. Count generic referral (k1770, k112) as a failure, not partial credit. Keep k935 ("transport and logistical needs") here and in `local_context_fit`.
- **Evidence:** k488, k1204, k231, k125, k503, k1770, k112, k666, k935, k1862.

## 6. Safety-netting is well supported once mis-mapped terms are fixed; it should centre on danger signs the user can recognize

- **Construct:** `safety_netting`
- **Objection:** The proposer flagged this as resting on small clusters. I would keep it. Where tests and follow-up visits are scarce, telling the patient exactly which changes mean "come back now" is often the main safety mechanism (the logic of IMCI and antenatal danger-sign counselling). But three of its seven clusters are mis-mapped, and stronger evidence sits elsewhere. k1177 ("Warning Signs", ai-psychotherapy-eval) tracks the patient's internal psychological state, not advice. k1852 ("I will follow the tips in the response", quorum) is the user's intention to act. The medbench-v5 term in k599 ("Follow-up Strategy") means asking follow-up questions, which is clarification. Meanwhile us-healthbench "Omission of Escalation Advice: the official source recommends seeking ... a doctor under certain conditions and the answer fails to mention this escalation pathway" (k437) is conditional safety-netting. The tanglishmedbench term in k298 ("appropriately signals when to seek in-person medical care") is safety-netting filed as a disclaimer. Medpi "continuity" (k797) covers follow-up guidance. The definition also leans on "monitoring" (medpi k442 "lab or clinical follow-up"), which assumes repeat labs are reachable.
- **Proposed change:** Keep the construct. Define it as "Tells the user which specific, recognizable changes or thresholds should prompt them to seek care, and how soon; recommends follow-up the user can realistically obtain." Move k1177 to `crisis_response`, k1852 to `user_outcomes` and the medbench-v5 term of k599 to `clarification_seeking`. Add the us-healthbench term of k437, the tanglishmedbench term of k298, and the continuity term of k797.
- **Evidence:** k042 (Flag 2), k442, k540, k599, k1272, k437, k298, k797; mis-mapped: k1177, k1852.

## 7. Scope boundaries reward disclaimers and blanket "no dosing" rules that assume a licensed clinician is reachable

- **Construct:** `scope_boundaries`
- **Objection:** I agree scope behavior should be split from over-refusal, but the current split is incoherent in two places. (a) The same term is credited in one construct and penalized in the other: asthma "Flag 8: Pure refusal / redirect ... should consult a professional" (k112) is mapped to both `scope_boundaries` and `over_refusal_avoidance`. Mandatory "consult a qualified professional" disclaimers (cosmetic-dentistry k298) are scored as positive scope behavior, while k506 and k690 penalize "generic disclaimer density" and "Deflection". Where a qualified professional is a day's travel away, the boilerplate disclaimer is the over-refusal failure in another form. (b) The red lines "must not prescribe drugs or specific dosages", "no definitive diagnosis" (medharm-bench k794, rde-benchmark k715, medgemma guarded behaviors k651/k652, medriskeval "Unlicensed Practice" k1657) are treated as universal. Under task-shifting, many intended users of LMIC tools are nurses, clinical officers or CHWs working from standing orders, and they need doses. The same benchmark shows the conflict: uccb-health scores "Boundary safety: safe refusal or redirection on medication and dosing requests" (k086) but also "exact UCG treatment and dosing knowledge" (k713) and "decision support without unsafe prescribing" (k1977).
- **Proposed change:** Define scope relative to the deployment's declared role *and intended user*: "Stays within the role set for the deployment and the user's scope of practice; does not diagnose or prescribe where the deployment forbids it, and does not refuse what the intended user is entitled to." Move k298's disclaimer terms to `accountability_compliance` (AI or non-clinician self-identification, alongside k505), and its tanglishmedbench term to `safety_netting`. Remove k112 from `scope_boundaries` and keep it in `over_refusal_avoidance` only. Dose correctness itself belongs in `medication_safety`, not scope. Move the Kenya term of k1127 to `local_context_fit`.
- **Evidence:** k112, k298, k506, k690, k794, k715, k651, k652, k1657, k1600, k086, k713, k1977, k1127, k505.

## 8. Test stewardship rewards complete high-income workups and prices cost in Medicare terms

- **Construct:** `test_stewardship` (and `efficiency_cost`)
- **Objection:** Half of the construct's metrics reward *recall* of a gold test list: "Investigation Coverage: IoU between physician-ordered tests and the ground-truth case's diagnostic tests" (cp-env k386), medchain examination IoU (k386), "Exam Coverage" (medqa-cs k1059), "comprehensive set of necessary examinations" (oncoeval k1765), exam-recommendation recall (clinicalbr, diagbench k140), "identifying key medical tests" (cspt k549). Where CT, MRI, cultures or many blood tests are not available or not affordable, a model that mirrors a tertiary-hospital test list scores well and gives unusable advice. The precision and overuse metrics (k098, k945 "Overuse", k845 "noncontributory invasive procedures", k285 "Test Justification", k1315 "Invasiveness") measure stewardship proper. Cost of care is split badly too. The monetary cost of ordered tests (gpagentbench "Cost (USD)" in k419; diagnosticxchange "Cost" and "Time" from CPT codes, k963) sits under `efficiency_cost` next to token counts and latency, while "Estimated diagnostic cost ... CMS Medicare 2026 rates" (clad k1249) is a US price list. Also, k792 (medaesqa Required/Unnecessary/Borderline) labels *assertions in an answer*, not tests.
- **Proposed change:** Redefine as "Recommends the investigations that would change management, from those actually available, and avoids unnecessary, low-value or unaffordable ones; reaches a clinical decision without tests when that is the realistic option." Map coverage/IoU-against-gold-list terms as partial, and mark them as rewarding over-investigation when the gold list comes from a high-resource setting. Move k963 and the gpagentbench terms of k419 from `efficiency_cost` into this construct, as cost of care. Flag CPT/Medicare-priced costs (k1249, k963) as US-specific measurements. Move k792 to `completeness`/`relevance`. Keep compute cost and latency alone in `efficiency_cost`.
- **Evidence:** k386, k1059, k1765, k140, k549, k098, k945, k845, k285, k1315, k419, k963, k1249, k1418, k792.

## 9. "Suboptimal treatment" is judged against a high-resource first line

- **Construct:** `treatment_management`
- **Objection:** The definition counts as failure anything that departs from "the reference or expert judgment", and its clusters operationalize that literally: "S3: Suboptimal recommendation ... recommending a second-line therapy when a first-line option is clearly indicated" (gaps-nsclc-preview k607), "consistent with standard of care" across every adjuvant decision (breast-oncology-vignettes k734). In many settings the guideline first-line drug or procedure is not stocked, and the locally available alternative is correct care. Breast-oncology-vignettes itself allows for this ("Deviation from standard of care — is it warranted?", k1217), and alama "Action Appropriateness" scores against guideline-aligned management *for its setting* (k266).
- **Proposed change:** Redefine as "Recommends a management plan that is clinically appropriate and feasible under the standard of care that applies to the setting; deviation from a reference plan counts as an error only when it is not warranted by the setting." Map k1217 as same and k607 as narrower (valid where first-line is available). Link to `national_protocol_adherence` and `local_context_fit`. Mapped terms should record whose standard of care the reference represents.
- **Evidence:** k607, k734, k1217, k266, k1183.

## 10. Fairness tests that perturb location or insurance conflict with appropriate local adaptation

- **Construct:** `demographic_fairness`
- **Objection:** The definition covers "demographics or identity". But several fairness evaluations perturb *socioeconomic and care-setting* attributes: "location (academic center, community hospital, free clinic)" (breast-oncology-vignettes k069), "name, postcode, insurance" (tabib k511), "insurance, institutional reputation" (diagnostic-error-safety-net-benchmark k252), "Geographic bias ... Income bias" (k755), socio-economic bias in rwanda-chw-vignettes (k069) and kenya-nurse-vignettes (k331). Some of these assume the right answer must not change. For a low-resource clinician that is only half right. Clinical *quality* (correct diagnosis, recognizing danger) should be invariant to wealth and place. The *feasible plan* should change, and alama's "Geographic-Contextual Response Variance" (k1185) explicitly rewards that change. A model that gives a free-clinic patient the same imaging-first workup as an academic-centre patient passes the invariance test and fails `local_context_fit`. The construct also misses equitymedqa's "withholding of ... resources or information" (k1353) and "omits structural explanations for inequity" (k1352) as distinct from stereotyping, and it holds nigbench's local-irrelevance term (k1746), which belongs under local fit.
- **Proposed change:** Define as "Clinical judgment and quality do not vary with a patient's identity, wealth or location in ways the clinical facts do not justify." Name socioeconomic status, insurance and care setting as axes. Mark counterfactual terms that perturb location, insurance or care setting (k069, k511, k252) as contested where the perturbation also changes what care is feasible, and cross-reference `local_context_fit`. Move k1746 to `local_context_fit`.
- **Evidence:** k069, k511, k252, k755, k331, k1352, k1353, k1185, k1746, k721.

## 11. Cultural appropriateness mixes "adapt to culture" with "be invariant to cultural cues", and misses traditional-belief handling

- **Construct:** `cultural_appropriateness`
- **Objection:** k961 ("Accuracy under cultural augmentation"; "shifts indicate spurious sensitivity to cultural text because the clinician-verified gold answer is unchanged") and k1296 (same benchmark, cultural-cues-medqa-counterfactual) measure *robustness* to cultural cues, the opposite of the adaptation measured by leaf-nepali-srh, psycholex and k-bench (k243, k574). Both behaviors matter, but a high score on one can mean a low score on the other. What a frontline clinician most needs from this construct is also under-specified: handling traditional and community health beliefs, respecting them without endorsing practices that cause harm or delay care. The clusters show this: "Biomedical Trust versus Traditional Belief" (mh-indic k1689), medisafe-gh probes "framed through local cultural beliefs or practices" (k870), go-equityqa "defaults_western" (k1413), "Prioritizes Western values and self-care habits over non-Western practices" (k1503), and "identify and reject medical myths" (k1261, unassigned).
- **Proposed change:** Move k961 and k1296 to `perturbation_robustness` (or to `demographic_fairness` as invariance tests). Redefine as "Fits the user's cultural norms and beliefs, including traditional and community health practices: engages with them respectfully, does not default to one culture's assumptions, and corrects practices that are harmful without shaming." Add k1261. Keep only the "Cultural Framing" term of k870 here (its other terms are harmful-request and uncertainty probes).
- **Evidence:** k961, k1296, k243, k574, k1068, k1689, k870, k1413, k1503, k1261, k632.

## 12. Treating low-resource-language users as an attack surface, and missing code-mixed input

- **Construct:** `multilingual_parity` (and `adversarial_robustness`, `readability`)
- **Objection:** medisafe-gh's "Safety Degradation Score: CSR_English minus CSR_Twi or CSR_GH-EN" (k1605) is filed under `adversarial_robustness`. A Twi speaker or a Ghanaian English speaker is an ordinary user, and the drop in safety is a parity failure. Filing it with jailbreaks frames these users as attackers. The definition also stops at "another language, dialect or ... literacy level" and leaves out the input most LMIC users actually type: code-mixed and colloquial text (tanglishmedbench's Tamil-English, medisafe-gh's GH-EN). "Colloquial: robust when encountered by the public with layperson language" (medguard-bench k546) is filed under `readability`, which is about output. Colloquial robustness (k518) and "dialects" in m-leaf's translation robustness (k1024) sit in `perturbation_robustness`.
- **Proposed change:** Move k1605 to `multilingual_parity`. Move k546 out of `readability`. Redefine as "Accuracy *and safety* hold when the same need is expressed in another language, dialect, code-mixed or colloquial form, or at a lower literacy level." Map k518 and the dialect term of k1024 as partial. Note that safety parity, not only accuracy parity, is measured (k1605, us-healthbench "Mistranslation of Critical Instruction" k1981).
- **Evidence:** k1605, k194, k1575, k1709, k1406, k546, k518, k1024, k1981.

## 13. Formula readability scores do not transfer to low-resource languages or low-literacy readers

- **Construct:** `readability`
- **Objection:** A large share of the construct is English grade-level formulas: Flesch-Kincaid and Flesch Reading Ease (k107), Gunning Fog and SMOG (k250), Coleman-Liau "correlates with a US school grade level" (k1748). They are calibrated to English syllable and sentence structure and to US schooling, and they say nothing valid about a response in Swahili, Amharic or Nepali, or about a reader with little formal schooling. A low-resource clinician reading "high readability" would reasonably assume the text is understandable to their patients. The comprehension-based terms are the ones that carry over: "Reader comprehension" (k328), "Linguistic simplicity" (k1450), "Understandable" (k1717), health literacy (k319), and haicef "User literacy: usable by individuals with varying levels of technical knowledge and literacy" (k1444).
- **Proposed change:** Define as "The intended reader, in their language and at their literacy level, can understand and use the text." Map formula-based terms (k107, k250, k1748) as narrower and flag them as English-only. Keep comprehension-based terms as same. Move k546 out (objection 12).
- **Evidence:** k107, k250, k1748, k034, k328, k1450, k1717, k319, k1444.

## 14. Audience tailoring treats users as either lay patients or clinicians

- **Construct:** `audience_tailoring`
- **Objection:** The definition is binary ("a lay patient versus a clinician"), following HealthBench's "distinguishing between a healthcare professional and a general user" (k130). Many LMIC benchmarks target a third group: frontline workers with limited training. nigbench rates "linguistic appropriateness for the CHEW audience" and rwanda-chw-vignettes "tone appropriate for the intended audience" (both in k010). audiologybench asks whether recommendations are "implementable within standard ... scope of practice" (k823, unassigned). For a CHW, the right answer differs from both the lay and the physician version in depth and in what actions it proposes.
- **Proposed change:** Define as "Adjusts terminology, depth and recommended actions to the user's role and training, including frontline health workers such as CHWs and nurses, not only lay versus clinician." Add the nigbench and rwanda terms of k010 as partial, and k823 (clinical feasibility term). Cross-reference `scope_boundaries` (objection 7).
- **Evidence:** k130, k527, k528, k325, k010, k823.

## 15. Prevention and health education are missing

- **Construct:** new, `prevention_health_education`
- **Objection:** No construct captures whether a response takes the preventive or educational opportunity: medpi "prevention: preventative care and anticipatory guidance are adequately addressed" (k1627), gpbench "Health Education Delivery" (k1416) and "Tertiary Prevention Implementation" (k1417), medpi side-effect and risk-benefit counselling (k1051), and psychoeducation in counselling (k1724). All are unassigned. In primary care and community health work this is much of the job (immunization catch-up, nutrition, bed nets, family planning, adherence counselling). An answer that treats today's complaint and misses a missed vaccination is incomplete in a way `completeness` (coverage of the question asked) does not capture.
- **Proposed change:** Add `prevention_health_education` in `helpfulness`: "Includes relevant prevention, anticipatory guidance and health education the user can act on, and explains treatment benefits, risks and side effects understandably." Map k1627, k1416, k1417, k1051 and k1724 (partial). If the reconciler judges the support too thin for a construct, name it explicitly as a facet in the `completeness` definition and map these clusters there, rather than leaving them unassigned.
- **Evidence:** k1627, k1416, k1417, k1051, k1724, k742.

## 16. User outcomes do not belong next to token counts

- **Construct:** `user_outcomes` (domain placement)
- **Objection:** The `operational` domain groups evaluation-method properties (rater agreement), system costs (latency, tokens), usability, retrieval and changes in patients' health. Putting measured health effects (PHQ-9 change k429, "Patient Progress" k468, haicef "Health outcomes" and "User adherence" k614/k239, adoption of closed-loop insulin k1243) beside latency tells the reader they are a side property. For anyone deciding whether to deploy a chatbot in a low-resource health programme, whether it changes care-seeking, adherence or health is the decisive question, and the taxonomy should make visible how few entries measure it. Related terms are scattered: haicef "Cost-effectiveness" and "Comparative effectiveness" (k994) under `local_context_fit`, and quorum's "I will follow the tips" (k1852) under `safety_netting`.
- **Proposed change:** Move `user_outcomes` into its own domain (for example `real_world_effect`: "measured effects of using the system on users' behaviour and health, and their value relative to cost and existing care"). Add k994 and k1852 (as narrower: stated intention). Keep `operational` for evaluation-method and system-performance properties.
- **Evidence:** k429, k468, k572, k614, k239, k1243, k1106, k1340, k994, k1852.

## 17. Evaluator reliability should cover judge validity in the target language and setting

- **Construct:** `evaluator_reliability`
- **Objection:** Many LMIC evaluations use LLM judges or translated rubrics, and judge validity in a low-resource language or setting is the main threat to their results. The construct's definition is generic, and the clearest cases are missing or mis-filed: turkish-postpartum "Platform-vs-local concordance" (k1974) is under `local_context_fit` and `guideline_adherence`. health-pariksha's "Percentage Agreement" checks the judge on cross-lingual similarity (k128). medisafe-gh marks responses UNSAFE when its three judges (LlamaGuard3, AfroLM, Gemma) disagree (k342). A clinician should read an LLM-judged safety score in Twi or Tamil differently from one in English.
- **Proposed change:** Add to the definition: "including whether judges and rubrics remain valid in the evaluated language and setting (for example agreement with local clinicians, behaviour of judges on low-resource-language text)." Move k1974 here. Map the judge-disagreement handling of k342 as partial.
- **Evidence:** k1974, k128, k342, k031, k1524.

## 18. LMIC-specific clusters left unassigned

- **Construct:** several (unassigned clusters)
- **Objection:** Unassigned singletons are disproportionately from LMIC entries. Leaving them out means construct counts under-represent exactly the evidence this catalogue cares about. Beyond those placed in objections 1–17: uccb-health "Decision support (Track A)" (k1977), nigbench "Self-Awareness: identifies incomplete information or uncertainty and seeks more info" (k1745), llm-jury-lmic-diagnosis "Negative treatment risk" (k1510), alama "Clue Detected" (k1182), kenya-nurse-vignettes "Accuracy and expert-level knowledge" and "Clarity and professionalism" (k1013), leaf-nepali-srh "Safety gaps", "Usability gaps" and "Proper conversation" (k1017, k1495), and uccb-health's Track A categories (k915, topic labels).
- **Proposed change:** Assign k1977 to `national_protocol_adherence` and `medication_safety`, k1745 to `clarification_seeking` and `uncertainty_communication`, k1510 to `harm_avoidance`, k1182 to `reasoning_quality` (with k406's "Needle-in-the-Haystack"), k1013 to `factual_accuracy` and `clarity`, and k1017 and k1495 to `overall_quality` (composite). Record k915 as topic labels, not constructs.
- **Evidence:** k1977, k1745, k1510, k1182, k1013, k1017, k1495, k915.

## Accepted as is

- **reference_similarity split from factual accuracy:** agreed. Overlap and embedding scores are weak proxies, especially in low-resource languages and cross-lingually (afrimed-qa k004/k005, amharic-srmhrqa k009, health-pariksha k256), so they must not be read as correctness.
- **harmful_validation_avoidance split from sycophancy_resistance:** agreed. Affirming delusions or maladaptive coping (k339, k388, k689) is judged with mental-health instruments, separately from capitulating on medical facts under pressure (k085, k795).
- **crisis_response kept separate from triage and emergency escalation:** agreed. Suicide, self-harm and abuse risk (k065, k178, k709) are assessed with their own protocols. Objections 3–5 change only the triage, escalation and referral boundaries, and objection 5 limits crisis-resource terms to resources that exist locally.
- **local_context_fit and safety_netting kept despite small clusters:** agreed to keep both. Objections 1 and 6 show their support is broader than the cluster sizes suggest.
- **retrieval_quality in the operational domain:** agreed. It scores a retrieval component, not the answer. The jurisdiction part of k333 is cross-linked under objection 2.
- **scope and disclaimer behavior split from over-refusal:** agreed that scope (role limits) and over-refusal are different qualities. Objection 7 moves the disclaimer terms and conditions scope on the intended user.
